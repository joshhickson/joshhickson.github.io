ProblemReport → Executive summary

The Problem

In 2026, memory is the constraint. Demand for long-context reasoning and agentic workloads has pulled DRAM and NAND supply toward the datacenter, and the price per gigabyte has followed. The effect on independent researchers has been direct.

The industry's answer for local inference is the NVIDIA DGX Spark. It is an excellent machine. It provides 128 GB of unified memory with over 500 GB/s of bandwidth, and it runs a 70-billion parameter model without complaint. It also costs $4,700.

We respect that machine. We could not afford one, and neither could most of the people we build for.

Insight

The Insight

Every inference workload has the same shape. A large body of weights, a key-value cache that grows with context, and a memory system that cannot hold both. The industry solves this by buying faster memory.

We looked at the other end of the table. Per gigabyte, the cheapest solid-state storage on the open market is not enterprise NVMe. It is the consumer MicroSD card — sold in forty-packs, stocked in every electronics aisle on earth, manufactured at a scale no enterprise part will ever reach.

We asked a simple question. What if you bought enough of them?

ArchitectureReport → Hardware architecture

The Architecture

The reference design is a 40-lane parallel SDIO array.

Forty independent buses. Each MicroSD socket gets its own SDIO interface, running 4-bit UHS-I at a 208 MHz clock. Six signals per card: clock, command, and four data lines. Nothing is shared, so nothing arbitrates.

240 high-speed traces, routed to spec. Forty cards times six signals is 240 traces that must reach the controller with their eye diagrams intact. The layout follows the 3W rule — adjacent traces separated by at least three times a single trace width — on signal layers backed by unbroken ground planes one dielectric away, with ground transition vias placed beside every signal via. Signal-integrity-forward, from the first revision.

Multiphase power delivery. Forty cards writing at once draw roughly 6 A on the 3.3 V rail, near 20 W for storage media alone. A single-phase converter droops under that load step, and a MicroSD controller that sees 3.3 V sag toward 2.7 V will brown-out reset mid-write. The reference design specifies a multiphase DC-DC converter that divides the load across timed power stages. Power-dense by design.

Local bulk capacitance. Every socket gets its own bank of multi-layer ceramic capacitors, placed adjacent to the card. Low ESR, low ESL, and close enough to answer a load step before the regulator has noticed one.

ThermalReport → Thermodynamic realities

Thermal

A MicroSD card puts a controller and several stacked NAND dies inside a 15 x 11 x 1 mm plastic shell. Plastic insulates. Under continuous I/O the die reaches its throttle threshold — 70 to 85 °C — and the controller drops a 104 MB/s card below 10 MB/s to protect itself.

The reference design specifies a direct-die thermal solution, applied by the builder. Sand the casing back to expose the die, apply thermal interface material, clamp the array under a finned aluminum heatsink, and move air across it. The procedure is documented. The grits are in the kit.

SoftwareReport → Operating system

Openfarce

The Linux kernel does not accept that an array of removable SD cards is memory, and on the evidence it is not wrong. Standard mmap() with swap on a high-latency block device produces swap thrashing: the kernel spends its cycles shuffling 4 KB pages, and token generation stops.

OpenFARCE bypasses legacy kernel assumptions. It is a user-space I/O layer. It locks the resident model with --mlock so the kernel cannot page it out, skips the VFS entirely, and issues asynchronous scatter-gather reads against the raw block devices. It reads the attention pattern to predict which KV blocks the next decode step will need, and prefetches them while the current token is still computing.

OpenFARCE is open source. Status: pre-alpha. It is not included in any kit.

QuantizationReport → Ternary quantization

Quantization

This is why it works.

BitNet-class ternary quantization restricts every weight to one of three discrete values. At 1.58 bits per weight, a 70-billion parameter model compresses from a native footprint near 140 GB to approximately 13.8 GB, holding near-parity with full-precision models on perplexity.

That number changes the architecture. 13.8 GB of weights fit in the DRAM of a budget developer board, which means the weights never stream from the array during decode. The array stops trying to be memory and becomes what it is actually good at — a large, sequential sink for KV cache, written in contiguous 256-token chunks.

EnduranceReport → The endurance crisis

Endurance

Consumable media architecture.

Consumer MicroSD cards are rated for 1,000 to 3,000 program/erase cycles. Under the fragmented write pattern of a swapping inference engine, the write amplification factor climbs to 10.0 or higher — every gigabyte of KV cache written costs ten gigabytes of endurance. Our analysis models a 64-card configuration study and projects that an array under continuous load exhausts itself in approximately 64 days.

We plan for that rather than around it. Media is a consumable in this architecture, the same way it is in a tape library. The Endurance Pack ships replacement cards.

Risks

Risks And Challenges

What you receive. Parts. The contents lists above are complete and exact — breakout boards, perfboard, wire, passives, a heatsink, cards. There is no printed circuit board in any kit. There is no enclosure. There is no firmware on a chip. You build the board on perfboard, from the open reference design, yourself.

Parts sourcing. Every item is a commodity part with a public datasheet. MicroSD prices moved roughly 30% month to month through 2026 and will move again. If a tier's parts cost rises past 70% of its price, we pause that tier rather than ship a thinner kit. If a tier becomes unfulfillable, we offer refunds before ordering anything.

Firmware timeline. OpenFARCE is pre-alpha and is not included in any kit. Nothing you receive will run a language model on arrival, and we will not give a date when it will.

The array is not expected to outlive its first quarter of use. That is the finding. We have not disputed it, and we have not designed around it.

Scale. One person packs and ships these from a fixed parts budget. Every physical tier is capped for that reason, and a capped-out tier closes.
