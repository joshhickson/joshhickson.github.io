Boilerplate, 100 words

Farce Systems builds open reference kits for memory-constrained machine learning. Its first design, the F.A.R.C.E. Flash — Flash Array for Resource-Constrained Environments — targets the 128 GB working set required by a 70-billion parameter model using a 40-lane parallel SDIO array of consumer MicroSD cards, at a fraction of the cost of the NVIDIA DGX Spark.

Farce Systems ships reference kits, not products: every component is an off-the-shelf part with a public datasheet, and the PCB design, assembly guide, and OpenFARCE firmware are open source. Before designing the array, Farce Systems commissioned an independent feasibility analysis and published it unedited, conclusion included.

(100 words)

Three headlines

  1. A hardware project wants to replace $4,700 of unified memory with forty MicroSD cards
  2. This startup commissioned a study of its own product. The study says it won't work. They published it anyway.
  3. Forty cards, 240 traces, and a 64-day lifespan: inside the F.A.R.C.E. Flash
